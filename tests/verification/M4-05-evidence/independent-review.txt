Six explanation topics
-----------------------
Each changed topic was read in both languages and inspected above and below the
graphic at 1440px. The material introduces the relevant concepts before the
graphic, and the section following it has a meaningful heading and consistent
alignment. The text no longer appears as an unrelated block appended to an image.

- Reading a spectrum: the two axes are introduced first, the graphic illustrates
  the distinction, and the final section develops the route from spectrum to
  metric. The highlighted formula and scale reminder belong visibly to that
  final section.
- Illuminance, EDI and DER: three introductory concept cards prepare the reader
  for the graphic. The following section distinguishes light level from spectral
  shape, then groups the formula, further context and limits.
- Light colour and colour rendering: the two introductory questions and paired
  definitions align with the graphic's comparison. The concluding section about
  unavailable values is a clear application question.
- Age and the eye: the reference scenario and two influences are explained
  before the illustration. The formula and model limitation are grouped as an
  interpretation section afterward.
- Import, scaling and export: the opening states the comparison task, source and
  level are introduced before the graphic, and the final numbered section deals
  with analysis/export and choosing the comparison basis.
- Steps and combined effects in the light path: saving and the comparison scope
  precede the graphic. The final section explains how to read the plot and groups
  its interpretation caveat with that guidance.

On wide screens, the concluding heading in the left column and related text in
the right column create a consistent reading structure. In the checked narrow
layouts, these sections stack in the expected order. Long German words, English
headings, formulas, cards and highlighted notes remain contained and readable.
This is a judgment of information order and presentation, not an independent
verification of physical statements, model outputs or numerical examples.
